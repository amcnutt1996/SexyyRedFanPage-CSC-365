Early Life

Before the Glo

Growing Up in Memphis Gloria Hallelujah Woods was born on July 28, 1999, in Memphis, Tennessee, the eighth of ten children. She grew up in one of America's most storied music cities — a place that shaped legends across soul, blues, and hip-hop — and that environment left a deep mark on who she would become as an artist. She was homeschooled until fifth grade before attending Melrose High School.

Church Choir to the Booth

Before she ever picked up a microphone as a rapper, Gloria sang in her church choir. Music was always part of her life, but her path changed direction after she lost her voice. Rather than stepping away from music altogether, she leaned into rap — and discovered a gift that would eventually take her to the top of the charts. She began rapping at age 16, soaking up the energy of Memphis street rap and the broader trap and drill movements sweeping the country.

Influences

GloRilla has cited Chief Keef as her favorite rapper and a major inspiration in developing her style. She also drew from the legacy of Memphis icons like Gangsta Boo, whose gritty, no-nonsense approach to rap helped pave the way for women in Southern hip-hop. These influences are baked into GloRilla's DNA as an artist — the aggression, the confidence, the regional pride.

Early Struggles

Success didn't come immediately. For years, GloRilla kept recording and releasing music independently while facing real financial hardship. She has spoken openly about losing her apartment, her cars, and nearly everything she had before her breakthrough. During that period she worked at a Checkers drive-thru in Memphis just to get by — a detail she has since embraced, even partnering with the fast-food chain years later after making it big. Her persistence through those years is a core part of her story and her appeal.

First Steps In Music

She released her debut mixtape, Most Likely Up Next, in 2019, followed by the EP P Status in 2020. Both projects built her a loyal local following but hadn't yet broken through nationally. That would come — and when it did, it came fast.
